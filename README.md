This project showcases advanced multiphysics simulation skills through the modeling and analysis of a piezoelectric force sensor using COMSOL Multiphysics. The work focuses on accurately capturing the direct and inverse piezoelectric effects, which are fundamental to microsensors and microactuators.

🔍 Project Highlights

Developed 2D and 3D FEM models of a PZT-based piezoelectric structure

Simulated direct piezoelectric effect: mechanical load → generated electric potential

Simulated inverse piezoelectric effect: applied voltage → mechanical deformation

Performed parametric sweeps over force and voltage to quantify electromechanical coupling

Compared 2D vs 3D results to understand dimensional and modeling trade-offs

Combined electrical and mechanical loading to meet nanometer-scale deformation constraints

🧠 Engineering Value

Demonstrates strong understanding of coupled electro-mechanical physics

Shows ability to design simulations under realistic performance constraints

Emphasizes result validation, interpretation, and design decision-making

Directly applicable to MEMS, microsensors, actuators, and R&D simulation roles

🛠 Tools & Techniques

COMSOL Multiphysics (Piezoelectricity & Structural Mechanics interfaces)

FEM meshing strategies (swept & mapped meshes)

Parametric studies, solver tuning, and result post-processing

This project reflects an industry-oriented simulation workflow—bridging physical theory, numerical modeling, and design optimization for sensor and actuator systems.
